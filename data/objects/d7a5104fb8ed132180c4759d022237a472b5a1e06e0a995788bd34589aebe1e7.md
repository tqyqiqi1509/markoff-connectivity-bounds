## 2. A self-contained half-line construction

Use the normalized Fourier transform \(\widehat f(u)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi iux}\,dx\). Set
\[
 K(x)=\left(\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}\right)^2,\qquad
 h(x)=xK(x),\qquad P_n(x)=h(x-n)-h(x)\quad(n\ge1).
\]
The removable values are always understood. The elementary Fourier pair is
\(\widehat K(u)=(1-|u|)_+\). It follows by differentiating this pair as a tempered distribution that, for \(0<|u|<1\),
\[
 \widehat P_n(u)=-\frac{i\operatorname{sgn}u}{2\pi}
 (e^{-2\pi inu}-1),
\]
with value zero at zero and outside \([-1,1]\), and continuous endpoint limits. The factor in parentheses removes every apparent endpoint jump.

For clarity, these paired terms are genuinely integrable. The identity
\[
 P_n(x)=\frac{n\sin^2\pi x}{\pi^2x(x-n)}
\]
shows bounded integrals in half-unit neighborhoods of 0 and n. In the remaining region \(|x|\le2n\), use \(n/(|x||x-n|)\le1/|x|+1/|x-n|\); outside it use \(2n/x^2\). Thus \(\|P_n\|_1\ll1+\log(n+1)\). Its integral is zero: integrating the translation difference over \([-R,R]\) leaves intervals of length n at the two ends, each tending to zero as \(R\to\infty\).

For \(a>0\) define
\[
 M_a(x)=K(x)+\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-an}\{K(x-n)-aP_n(x)\},
 \qquad L_a=M_a-K.
\]
The exponential coefficients and the preceding bound give convergence in \(L^1\). Local uniform convergence also follows from the explicit removable rational factors. The Fourier transform is continuous and supported in \([-1,1]\). Fourier inversion therefore writes this continuous function as an integral over that compact interval, giving an entire extension of exponential type at most \(2\pi\). This argument avoids a separate appeal to a general Paley–Wiener theorem.

Let \(E_a(x)=e^{-ax}\) for \(x>0\), \(E_a(x)=0\) for \(x<0\), and \(E_a(0)=1/2\). Put
\[
 B(w)=\frac{w}{1-e^{-w}},\qquad B(0)=1.
\]
The elementary inequalities \(e^w\ge1+w\) and \(e^{-w}\ge1-w\) give \(0<B'(w)<1\), with \(B'(0)=1/2\). Direct series integration gives, for nonzero x,
\[
 M_a(x)-E_a(x)=\frac{\sin^2\pi x}{\pi^2}
 \begin{cases}
 \int_0^\infty[B(a+w)-B(a)]e^{xw}\,dw,&x<0,\\
 \int_{-\infty}^0[B(a)-B(a+w)]e^{xw}\,dw,&x>0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here is the positive-axis derivation, so the displayed identity is not an unverified imported lemma. Substitute \(u=a+w\), split the resulting integral at zero, and use
\(B(u)=-u\sum_{n\ge1}e^{nu}\) for \(u<0\), and
\(B(u)=u\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-nu}\) for \(u>0\). Integrating the positive terms first gives
\[
 \int_{-\infty}^0 B(u)e^{xu}\,du=\sum_{n\ge1}(x+n)^{-2},
\]
while each term on \([0,a]\) equals
\(e^{a(x-n)}[a/(x-n)-(x-n)^{-2}]+(x-n)^{-2}\).
Using \(\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(x-n)^{-2}=\pi^2/\sin^2\pi x\) and \(B(a)-a=a\sum_{n\ge1}e^{-an}\) gives the defining series of \(M_a-E_a\). The negative-axis calculation uses the positive-u geometric series directly. Positive-term convergence or exponential decay justifies the indicated exchanges; integer x follows by continuity.

Since \(0<B'<1\), each integral lies between zero and the same integral with the bracket replaced by \(|w|\). The latter is \(1/x^2\). Consequently
\[
 0\le M_a(x)-E_a(x)\le K(x),\qquad L_a(x)\le E_a(x)\le M_a(x).
\]
At zero, \(M_a(0)=1\) and \(L_a(0)=0\), so the asserted inequalities hold there too.

Termwise integration, justified by \(L^1\) convergence, yields
\[
 \int M_a=\frac1{1-e^{-a}},\qquad
 \int L_a=\frac1{e^a-1}.
\]
No statement about optimality is needed.

## 3. Closed finite windows

Let \(\sigma,L,\Lambda>0\), \(\Delta=\Lambda/(2\pi)\), \(a=\sigma/\Delta\), and \(\rho=e^{-\sigma L}\). Scale the half-line functions by \(M(v)=M_a(\Delta v)\) and \(L_*(v)=L_a(\Delta v)\). Then
\[
 N(v)=M(v)-\rho L_*(v-L)
\]
is a nonnegative majorant of \(e^{-\sigma v}{\bf1}_{[0,L]}(v)\), of angular Fourier support \([-\Lambda,\Lambda]\). Away from the endpoints this follows by subtracting a minorant of the shifted tail. At v=0, \(M(0)=1\) and \(L_*(-L)\le0\), so \(N(0)\ge1\). At v=L, \(M(L)\ge\rho\) and \(L_*(0)=0\), so \(N(L)\ge\rho\). Thus the closed interval is covered despite the averaged jump convention in \(E_a\).

Writing \(A=(1-\rho)/\sigma\), the nonnegative error has mass
\[
 E_{\rm win}=\frac1\Delta\left[
 \frac1{1-e^{-a}}-\frac{\rho}{e^a-1}\right]-A
 =\frac1\Delta[\rho+(1-\rho)g(a)],
\quad g(a)=\frac1{1-e^{-a}}-\frac1a.
\]
In particular \(0<E_{\rm win}<1/\Delta\), and the elementary transform estimate gives
\[
 |\widehat N(t)|\le
 \min\left(A,\frac{1+\rho}{|t|}\right)+E_{\rm win}.
\]
Reflection supplies the increasing exponential window on \([-L,0]\) required by the divisor recurrence, with unchanged modulus and normalization.

## 4. Cheap rational error bound and existing-prefix reuse

For a>0,
\[
 g(a)\le\frac12+\frac a{12}.
\]
Indeed, after multiplication by positive denominators this is equivalent to
\[
 (a^2-6a+12)e^a-(a^2+6a+12)
 =\sum_{n\ge5}\frac{(n-3)(n-4)}{n!}a^n\ge0.
\]
Also \(g(a)\le1\), from \(e^a-1\ge a\). Therefore one may use
\[
 E_{\rm win}\le\frac{2\pi}{\Lambda}
 \min\left\{1,\frac{1+\rho}{2}
       +\frac{(1-\rho)\pi\sigma}{6\Lambda}\right\}.
\]
This changes only the coefficient of the ordinary prefix \(I_0(\Lambda)\). The mass/harmonic split remains \(t_*=(1+\rho)/A\), so the already audited I0/I1 prefix structure is reusable at fixed epsilon and sigma. It does not justify reusing G nodes after those parameters change.

For outward arithmetic, write \(c=\pi\sigma/(6\Lambda)\). In the useful case c<1/2 the affine factor is increasing in rho; upper bounds for rho and c are safe. If an upper bound for c is at least 1/2, use the original factor 1. Do not substitute an upper rho into a decreasing affine expression without this case split. The numerical probes found the rational upper bound and exact mass indistinguishable for the selected bandwidths at the displayed precision.

## 5. Explicit Fourier kernel

For 0<u<1 put \(Q(u)=(1-e^{-a-2\pi iu})^{-1}\), \(Q_0=(1-e^{-a})^{-1}\). Direct transformation of the paired series gives
\[
 \widehat M_a(u)=(1-u)Q(u)+\frac{ia}{2\pi}[Q(u)-Q_0],
 \qquad \widehat L_a(u)=\widehat M_a(u)-(1-u).
\]
For angular t in (0,Lambda), use u=t/Lambda and
\[
 \widehat M(t)=\Delta^{-1}\widehat M_a(u),\qquad
 \widehat N(t)=(1-\rho e^{-itL})\widehat M(t)
             +\rho e^{-itL}\Delta^{-1}(1-u).
\]
At t=0 the continuous value is \(A+E_{\rm win}\); at t=Lambda it is zero. The sign of the imaginary term follows from \(\widehat{xK}=i\widehat K'/(2\pi)\); reflection conjugates the formula. The FLOAT code also tested the distinct weighted-Selberg mixture kernel from the root proof. It minimizes whole integrals over valid majorants; it does not take a pointwise minimum across transforms belonging to different majorants.
